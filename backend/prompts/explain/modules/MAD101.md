If the question is about a graph or tree shown as a drawing, add ONE fenced code block with the language `dot` right after the opening paragraph, reproducing the graph exactly as drawn in the image so the student can compare the two.
- Same structure: keep the vertex labels exactly as in the image (never rename or renumber), every edge, every direction (`digraph` with `->` for arrows, otherwise `graph` with `--`), every weight as `label`, loops, multiple edges and edge styles (dashed, bold). Never add, drop, merge or move an edge.
- Same layout: start with `layout=neato;` and pin every vertex with `pos="x,y!"` at its position in the image (x grows to the right, y grows upward, in inches: about 1 inch between neighbouring vertices, at most 6 wide), so the drawing looks like the one in the exam and edges cross where they cross in the image.
- Highlight what the answer relies on (a path, circuit, spanning tree, a vertex) with `color=red, penwidth=2`. Highlighting is the only thing you may add to the image.
- Faithfulness matters more than appearance: never draw an edge, weight or direction you cannot read. Before writing the block, check it against the image (vertex count, each edge once, directions, weights, and that the degrees add up to twice the number of edges). If you cannot reproduce the graph with confidence, omit the block.
- Keep it small (about 25 vertices at most), plain DOT only: no HTML labels, no `URL`, `href` or `image` attributes.
- Do not explain DOT or mention the block; just refer to "đồ thị" in the Vietnamese text.
</graphs>
