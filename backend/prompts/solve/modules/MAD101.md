<module key="MAD101">
<profile>
Discrete Mathematics, following Rosen's "Discrete Mathematics and Its Applications": logic and proofs, sets, functions and sequences, algorithms, number theory and cryptography, induction and recursion, counting, recurrence relations, graphs, and trees (traversals, spanning trees, minimum spanning trees). Questions are computations or short proofs with numeric or symbolic options. An exam normally contains one image that is a formula and reference sheet instead of a question; its position varies.
</profile>

<rules>
1. Formula sheet: formulas, definitions or tables with no question stem and no lettered options. Mark it "not_question" and never turn its formulas into separate questions. If it is in the same request, use its notation and formulas as the reference for the other questions.
2. Read symbols exactly: subscripts, summation limits, the direction of implications, membership versus subset, C(n,k) versus P(n,k), look-alike characters (0/O, 1/l, x/×).
3. Graphs and trees (accuracy is critical: a wrongly transcribed graph gives a wrong answer). Transcribe the drawing into Graphviz DOT (`graph` or `digraph`) before solving, then verify the DOT against the image:
   a. List the vertices with their exact labels as drawn (never rename or renumber them) and count them.
   b. Go through the image edge by edge and tick each one in the DOT, counting every edge once. A missing, extra or reversed edge is the usual error.
   c. Confirm directions, weights, loops and multiple edges.
   d. Check that the degrees add up: the sum of degrees equals twice the number of edges (for a digraph, the in-degrees and the out-degrees each sum to the number of edges).
   Fix the DOT until every check passes and compute only from the verified DOT, never from the picture. If you cannot read an edge, a weight or a direction with confidence, do not guess it: mark the question "unsure".
4. Math logic is unforgiving, so check every result twice. Compute exactly (keep fractions, radicals and large integers exact; round only when asked), then verify by an independent route (substitute into the options, test a small case, or use a different method) and re-read the question for qualifiers (NOT, EXCEPT, "at least") before committing. If nothing matches the options, re-check your reading once, then take the closest option as "unsure".
5. Options are often near misses (off by one, wrong sign, P instead of C, a missing factor): compare every option with your exact result. Labels can go beyond D, and an option such as "None of the other choices is correct" is right only after you have checked that every other option is false.
</rules>

<examples>
<example name="formula_sheet">
Input: an image listing C(n,k)=n!/(k!(n-k)!), the inclusion-exclusion formula and Euler's formula, with no question and no options.
Handling: there is nothing to answer, so do not make questions out of the formulas.
Output: kind="not_question", choice="", confidence="certain".
</example>
<example name="dense_but_a_question">
Input: an image with a table of set identities and the stem "Which identity is NOT valid?" with options A to D.
Handling: it has a stem and options, so it is a question; the table is part of it.
Output: kind="question", choice=the letter of the invalid identity.
</example>
<example name="near_miss_options">
Input: "How many 3-letter strings can be formed from the 26 letters with no letter repeated?" Options: A. 17576, B. 15600, C. 2600, D. 325.
Handling: order matters and repetition is forbidden, so P(26,3) = 26*25*24 = 15600. 17576 = 26^3 is the repetition-allowed trap; 2600 = C(26,3) and 325 = C(26,2) are combination slips.
Output: kind="question", choice="B", confidence="certain".
</example>
<example name="implication_direction">
Input: "Which statement is the contrapositive of p -> q?" Options include q -> p, not p -> not q, not q -> not p, p -> not q.
Handling: the contrapositive is not q -> not p; q -> p is the converse and not p -> not q is the inverse.
Output: kind="question", choice=the letter of "not q -> not p".
</example>
<example name="graph_to_dot">
Input: a drawing of an undirected graph; the question asks whether it has an Euler circuit.
Handling: transcribe it first as `graph G { A -- B; B -- C; C -- D; D -- A; A -- C; }`, then verify: 4 vertices; the 5 edges ticked one by one against the image; degrees A=3, B=2, C=3, D=2 add up to 10 = 2 x 5. Two vertices have odd degree, so there is no Euler circuit (an Euler path exists).
Output: kind="question", choice=the letter of "No".
</example>
<example name="none_of_the_other_choices">
Input: "Which relation on {1, 2, 3} is an equivalence relation?" Options A to D each list a relation; option E reads "None of the other choices is correct".
Handling: test reflexive, symmetric and transitive for each relation. If every one of A to D fails at least one property, E is the answer.
Output: kind="question", choice="E".
</example>
</examples>
